Building a strong activities list

A few things that consistently make an activities list stronger, regardless of which activity it is:

1

Depth beats a long list

Three activities you can talk about in real detail read stronger than ten you can't.

2

Say what you actually did

"Member" tells them nothing. What did you build, organize, fix, or lead?

3

Numbers where you have them

Hours per week, people involved, money raised, whatever is real and specific.

4

Don't inflate a short activity

Something you did for a month should read as a month. Reviewers can tell, and honesty reads better than exaggeration.

Recommendation Letters & Transcripts

Doing this without it being word-of-mouth

Asking for a recommendation letter
1

Ask early

Give your teacher at least 3–4 weeks before your earliest deadline. Teachers write many of these — the earlier you ask, the more thought yours gets.

2

Ask in person first

A quick conversation before the formal request feels far less transactional, and gives them a chance to say yes properly.

3

Give them material to work with

A short note on: what you're applying to study, one or two specific moments from their class you'd want mentioned, and your resume/activities list. It's easier for them to write something specific if you hand them specifics.

4

Follow up once, politely

A short check-in a week or two before the deadline is normal and expected — it's not rude.

Transcripts

The exact steps depend on how our school's administration office handles requests — that part needs confirming directly with them rather than guessed at here. What's safe to say generally: request it well before your first deadline, confirm whether it's sent electronically or needs to be mailed, and double-check the receiving school actually got it.

Scholarships

Funded routes for Saudi students

Custodian of the Two Holy Mosques Scholarship Program

The Saudi government's own scholarship program for study abroad, run by the Ministry of Education. Covers undergraduate and postgraduate study across several tracks (top-30 global institutions, promising sectors, and more).

Official program page
Ministry of Education scholarship portal
moe.gov.sa
Aramco — College Degree Program (CDPNE)

A selective scholarship for Saudi high school graduates, including from international curriculum schools. Includes a preparatory year in Dhahran before sponsoring study at a university abroad, with a job at Aramco after graduation.

Program page
Aramco careers — student opportunities
aramco.com
SABIC Scholarship Program

Sponsors outstanding Saudi high school graduates to study abroad, mainly in engineering, science, and business-related fields, through partner universities and preparatory programs.

Note: SABIC's scholarship program is administered through partner universities and changes year to year — search "SABIC scholarship [current year]" or check with a partner university (e.g. Prince Mohammad Bin Fahd University) directly for this cycle's application process.
KAUST Gifted Student Program (KGSP)

A fully-funded scholarship for Saudi students pursuing STEM undergraduate degrees abroad. Important: this one is mostly by invitation/nomination — through Mawhiba, MiSK, SRSI, or the International Olympiad Program — not a normal open application. If you haven't been nominated, there's still an interest form you can fill out.

KGSP official page
Program details and interest form
kgsp.kaust.edu.sa

Scholarship programs change eligibility, deadlines, and tracks year to year. Treat this page as a starting point, not a final answer — confirm current details on each program's own site.
